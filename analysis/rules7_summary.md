# RULES-7 — plain-language summary (for the in-game How-to-Play + GAME_DESIGN core loop)

1. Capturing an enemy tile buries it beneath yours — it never comes back, and your card is always spent. No more free "peeling" a tower off for nothing.
2. You can only capture a tile if you already have a tile next to it. Armies have to physically meet before anyone can strike — no more sniping from across the map.
3. Height matters: a taller stack presses down harder on shorter neighbors, and takes less pressure from below. High ground is a real, defensible advantage.
4. Stacking on your own tile still builds height, but no longer adds influence by itself. Only burying an ENEMY tile beneath you (a trophy) makes you stronger — up to 3 trophies per cell.
5. Stacks can grow 5 tiers tall through repeated fighting; beyond that the oldest buried tier crumbles to rubble — a battle scar, not a game effect.
6. The rift is fair ground worth holding: every cell on or beside the rift you control adds bonus "Riftlight" to your score at game's end.
7. Every 12 turns the rift stirs — tiles beside it take a hit (or a boost, if rift-attuned) for one round each, telegraphed two turns ahead.
8. Capitals can no longer hide in a board corner — a legal capital cell needs at least 3 open neighbors.
9. Ruins and decay are gone. A captured cell just gets a rubble marker for the art, with no lasting influence penalty.
10. Sunder now also needs you to have a tile next to its target — the same "you have to be there" rule as ordinary captures.
11. Ward still works exactly once per tile: the first capture attempt against a warded tile bounces the attacker's card back at no other cost. It's the only bounce left in the game.
12. Four new keywords put the height and rift systems on cards you'll actually draw: Surefoot ignores the uphill penalty, Tidebound grows from rift-side kills, Summit rewards a tier-3+ tower, Seambound rewards garrisoning the rift hex itself.
13. The starter deck now ships Flank, Wing, Sustain, a Sunder, and a Rallying Cry from turn one — the tension mechanics are visible in your very first game, not buried in the collection.
14. Deck size stayed at 20, not the 24 originally proposed — testing showed 24 pushed games past the target length; the extra card slots went to variety, not just more cards.
15. Net effect: captures per game roughly doubled, the share of games with zero captures fell from about half to under 5%, and matches reliably reach a real fight well before either deck runs dry.
