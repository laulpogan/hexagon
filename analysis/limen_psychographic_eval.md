Run of the `psychographic-eval` skill, game-design lens (MtG psychographics + Quantic Foundry +
Lazzaro). Pre-committed eval questions (before scoring): *which player type does Limen bore or
alienate, and where is the coverage gap that keeps fun at 6/10?*

## The core finding

**Limen serves the *aesthetic* axis (Melvin, Vorthos) and starves the *motivational* axis (Timmy,
Johnny, Spike).** The game looks and reads beautifully and interlocks elegantly — then plays hollow,
because the three types who actually drive *engagement and retention* each get bored. That is a
precise, mechanical explanation for "stuck at 6/10 fun": we polished the two types that admire the
game and neglected the three types that *play* it.

Ranked by starvation: **Timmy (worst) > Johnny > Spike.** Notably these three map onto the exact
levers the operator asked for in round 8 — which is strong confirmation the round-8 wishlist is
aimed at the real gap, not noise:

- **Timmy** ← big-turn economy, instants/sorceries, escalating events, wow-juice.
- **Johnny** ← on-placement effects, road chains, trap tiles, timing windows (the combo surface).
- **Spike** ← traps + telegraphed threats + timing → raise the solvable-at-depth-2 ceiling.

## Quantic Foundry cross-check
Served today: **Strategy** (Mastery), **Fantasy/Story** (Immersion). Starved: **Excitement/
Destruction** (Action — no swings), **Power/Completion** (Achievement — no ramp, weak progression
*feel* in-match), **Design/Discovery** (Creativity — shallow combos). RULES-8's economy+instants+
traps light up Action + Achievement + Creativity — the three unlit clusters.

## Lazzaro 4 Keys cross-check
Present: **Serious Fun** (the lore/meaning) and a thin **Hard Fun** (some challenge). Missing:
**Easy Fun** (novelty/spectacle turn-to-turn) and strong **Hard Fun** (a ceiling worth mastering).
Best-sellers ship ≥3 keys; Limen ships ~1.5. The Fun Index should include an Easy-Fun proxy
(spectacle/variance) and a Hard-Fun proxy (skill-depth band), not just Drama.

## Coverage gap + sharpest conflict
- **Gap:** the entire motivational axis. Fixing it is the whole point of round 8.
- **Sharpest conflict (the antithesis's warning, in psychographic terms):** serving Timmy+Johnny with
  a pile of new systems (traps, instants, economy, roads) risks **alienating Melvin** — sprawl breaks
  the elegant interlock — and busting the 25–45-ply target that keeps Spike's games tight. The
  synthesis must add motivational depth **without** shattering the Melvin elegance. That is the
  single hardest constraint on RULES-8, and it's why the dialectic (maximalist vs. one-bold-primitive)
  matters: the win condition is *Timmy+Johnny+Spike lit up while Melvin stays delighted.*

## One change, best appeal/cost ratio
If forced to pick one: **a shared escalating resource that turns roads into multi-placement chains and
funds instants** — it lights Timmy (big turns), Johnny (combo timing), and Spike (resource-management
depth) at once, off a *single* new primitive, which is the cheapest way to protect Melvin. This is
exactly the tension the round-8 antithesis is arguing; the psychographic math says the antithesis's
"one unifying primitive" is the right spine and the thesis's individual levers hang off it.
